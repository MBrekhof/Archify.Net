Renting in a big city for the first time: what should you confirm before signing?

Long image

A

Answer

It comes down to three things: confirm the person, the flat and the money. Verify the landlord's identity and ownership, write the key terms into the contract, and keep evidence of every payment and the handover.

  1. Check the landlord's identity and ownership before signing; do not rely on verbal promises.
  2. Rent, deposit, term, penalties and repair duties must be in the contract.
  3. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and inspect and photograph every item at handover.

  1. View the flat ↗

    Visit once in the evening and once at the weekend; test light, noise, water pressure and phone signal; check the commute.

  2. Check identity and ownership ↗

    See the original ID card and the property certificate; confirm the signer is the owner, or an agent with the owner's power of attorney.

  3. Write terms into the contract ↗

    To avoid disputes, put the key promises into the contract or an addendum, confirmed by both sides.

  4. Pay and keep receipts ↗

    Note the purpose on each transfer; ask for a receipt, or have the contract state the amount received.

  5. Inspect and photograph ↗

    Photograph the water, power and gas meter readings, the furniture and appliances, and any existing damage.

What do you check on signing day?

Landlord's ID card
Check the original; the name matches the property certificate
Property certificate
Check the address and the owner
Signing through an agent
Requires a power of attorney signed by the owner
First payment
Deposit plus the first rent period, amount written into the contract
Agency fee
Often half a month to a month's rent; agree it in advance (rule of thumb)

Walk away if you see these

Photos only, no viewing allowed, and pressure to pay a holding fee first. No proof of ownership, or a sublessor without the owner's written consent to sublet. Cash or a transfer to a third party's account demanded, with no receipt. A newly renovated flat with a sharp smell, or an obvious partitioned or overcrowded unit.
